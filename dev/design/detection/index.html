    Star and streak detection — design¶

    Crate fayde-detect. Survey of existing methods: docs/research/literature-review.md §7. Sources of every algorithm: docs/references.md.

    1. Scope¶

    Input: one image whose stars are points (a frame tracked at the sidereal rate, or a spacecraft holding its attitude), with possibly a satellite streak or two, hot pixels, cosmic-ray hits, saturated stars, stray light. Output: what the solver's input contract asks of a source list (plate-solver.md §6.3), filled honestly:

    Per source How it is obtained
    x, y Closed-form fit to the peak of the matched-filtered image (§3.4)
    sigma_px Noise theory of that estimator, per source (§4)
    flux Integral of the fitted Gaussian
    p_star Local false discovery rate × shape class (§3.8)
    truncated The fit window reaches the frame edge

    Streaks are traced and reported (ends, angle, width, flux), and every detection on one is kept out of the star list. Frames whose stars are trailed (tracking a satellite) are out of scope: their star list still comes from the host's own detector, as before.

    Design targets, in order: no parameter the user must know (star width, noise and threshold are measured or derived; gain is read from the header when it is there); robust to cosmic rays by the hundred and to stray light; position errors that the solver's verification and fit can trust; speed; and the same bits on every machine (fayde_core::Portable, no platform maths).

    2. Why not an existing detector¶

    From the survey (literature review §7), what is in use today:

    • None measures the star width and adapts its filter. astrometry.net's simplexy smooths with a fixed σ = 1 px; DAOFIND and photutils need the FWHM from the user; SExtractor a fixed 3 × 3 kernel; cedar-detect a fixed 7-pixel gate with manual binning.
    • None reports a per-star position error to the matcher.
    • None runs a streak detector before solving. Streak fragments are rejected only as a side effect of size or shape gates, and astrometry.net removes only horizontal and vertical lines.
    • Hot pixels and cosmic rays are handled by minimum-area or sharpness heuristics that conflict with undersampled stars.
    • Licences: SExtractor GPL, SEP LGPL, astrometry.net GPL as distributed, cedar-detect source-available but not open source. Everything here is written from the papers, so no other project's code, and none of its licence terms, enters Fayde.

    3. Method¶

    image
  │ 1. matched filter (Gaussian; width measured, image binned if stars are wide)
  │ 2. background and noise mesh of the filtered image
  │ 3. peaks: local maxima above the false-alarm threshold
  │ 4. one closed-form fit per peak → position, size, shape, amplitude
  │ 5. local test: each peak against the ring around it
  │ 6. classes: star, saturated, extended, sharp
  │ 7. streaks: traced from ridge-like peaks
  │ 8. probabilities and errors
  ▼
stars (x, y, σ, flux, p_star) + streaks

    3.1 Matched filter and scale¶

    The image is convolved with a Gaussian of the stars' width, the filter of highest signal-to-noise ratio for a point source in white noise. The width is not asked for. A first pass at σk = 1.2 px (within 7% of the best ratio for stars of FWHM 1.9 to 4.1 px) measures it (§3.3); a second pass is run only when the ratio 2·σk·σp / (σk² + σp²) falls below 0.93. Stars wider than σ = 2 px are binned first (block means), so wide-star frames cost less than narrow-star ones. At most three passes.

    3.2 Background and noise¶

    Tiles of 64 px (the same sky area on a binned image, down to 24 binned px), measured on the filtered image so that the noise is that of the thresholded quantity whatever the input's noise correlation.

    • Background: median of a sample of the tile, clipped once at median + 3σ.
    • Noise: 1.4826 × MAD / √2 of the differences of pixel pairs separated by more than the kernel's reach, clipped up to three times. A difference cancels the background and its gradient, which a spread of values would count as noise.
    • A tile standing above what its neighbours predict (a bright star, a galaxy) is replaced by the prediction: the largest, over the four lines through the tile, of the mean of its two neighbours on that line. SExtractor's 3 × 3 median flattens the corner of a frame lit from outside; this filter does not (the glow of stray light curves up along the direction to its source and down across it, and the line nearest that direction predicts at least the tile).
    • Between tile centres the values are bilinear; beyond the outermost centres the background is extrapolated in a straight line, so a gradient running off the frame is followed to the edge.

    Clipped sky. If at least a quarter of the valid raw pixels equal the frame minimum, Fayde checks the distribution of those pixels in each tile. A tile is padding if at least 99% of its valid pixels equal the floor, or if transitions between floor and above-floor pixels occur at most a quarter as often as in a random mix. The detector checks the transition rate in tiles with at least eight floor pixels.

    Fayde uses the clipped-sky model when more than half of the sky tiles with at least 24 valid pixels yield a fit. To fit a tile, at least a quarter of its valid pixels must be at the floor and at least 16 must be above it. The singly left-censored normal fit uses the floor fraction and an empirical quantile near the middle of the surviving values. Tiles without a fit use the frame's typical fit. When the model applies, floor pixels in padding tiles are undefined for detection. Otherwise, Fayde uses the standard filtered mesh.

    The fit estimates the sky before clipping. Its clipped moments give the background and noise of the recorded pixels. Fayde propagates these through the point filter and accounts for the skew of the filtered noise when setting the peak threshold. On a simulated frame of 100 stars with 90% of the sky clipped at zero, 95 of 579 detections were stars before; with the model, 100 of 100, and every star is found. On 35 real frames with 25–36% of their pixels at zero, every frame still solves and the fit residual fell on 31 of them (median 0.40 to 0.35 px).

    3.3 Star width¶

    For a peak of the filtered image, the variance of the star's profile is the fitted variance (§3.4) less the kernel's. The frame's star width is taken from bright, round peaks, but they are not all stars: on a warm or irradiated detector hot pixels and cosmic-ray hits outnumber stars ten to one or more. Nothing that came through the optics is sharper than the optics allow, and stars share one width where artefacts have many. So the stars are the widest distinct mode of the size distribution: in a histogram of ln(size), the widest local maximum holding at least 5% of the tallest one's count and separated from narrower sizes by a dip to half its height. Blends and galaxies form a tail, not a mode.

    The stars' shape is measured with their width: the median narrow-axis variance and the median axis ratio of the peaks in that mode. A drift during the exposure smears every star alike. Each source's narrow axis is then well under the stars' mean width, so narrowness (§3.6) and elongation (§3.6, §3.7) are judged against what the frame's stars show, not against a round star of the mean width.

    3.4 One fit per peak¶

    A quadratic surface is fitted by least squares to the logarithm of the 3 × 3 filtered pixels around the maximum. For an elliptical Gaussian this is exact, and it gives at once:

    • the position (no pixel-phase bias, unlike a quadratic fitted to the values themselves, whose bias reaches 0.03 px for these widths);
    • the variances along both axes and the orientation (eigen-decomposition of the fitted Hessian H: variance along an axis = −1/eigenvalue);
    • the amplitude, and so the flux 2π·A·σmajor·σminor.

    Cost: nine logarithms and about 40 multiplications per peak.

    3.5 Local test¶

    The mesh follows the frame on the scale of its tiles. Stray light and halos change faster, and what the mesh misses is left as broad bumps. A star is narrow: three widths from its centre nothing of it is left, so its height above the median of a ring of that radius (eight samples, robust to a neighbour or a streak crossing the ring) is its height; a bump two or more star-widths across falls short. Where the ring agrees with the mesh, the mesh (less noisy) stands; elsewhere the ring is the background and the peak must exceed it by the threshold less one σ. Saturated stars are exempt.

    3.6 Classes¶

    Class Test Goes to the solver
    sharp Narrowest axis under 0.4 of the stars' narrow-axis variance, beyond the fit's noise; or, in the unfiltered pixels, the second faintest of the four neighbours of the brightest pixel under half of exp(−1/σ²) of it (light through the optics reaches all four; charge deposited in the detector does not) no
    saturated Top at the saturation level (given, from the header, or the level of flat tops at the frame's brightest) yes, p × 0.9
    extended Elongated over 1.5 times the stars' own axis ratio, or over twice the stars' variance, beyond the fit's noise yes, p × 0.5
    streak On a traced streak no
    star The rest yes

    A saturated star's centre is the mean position of the core of its saturated region: the pixels whose runs of saturated pixels along the row and along the column are each at least half the longest. A bleed trail, a few pixels wide, has short runs across it and does not move the centre. A saturated region that is a line of even width is a streak, not a star.

    The work is bounded whatever the frame and the settings: each saturated pixel is flooded once per frame, a region is followed for at most 200,000 pixels, a saturation level within 20σ of the background is ignored, at most 200,000 peaks are measured and 1,000 streaks traced, and binning never leaves an image under 16 pixels on a side.

    3.7 Streaks¶

    A peak on a streak is a ridge: the fitted surface curves down across the streak and hardly at all along it. Each ridge-like peak (axis ratio at least twice the stars') seeds a trace: the ridge is followed one pixel at a time in both directions along a straight line refitted by least squares as points are added, re-centring on the brightest of five samples across the track, bridging gaps in proportion to the length traced. Holding the trace to a line keeps it on course past a bright star beside the streak or another streak crossing it. Then:

    • isolated points at the ends, short groups beyond a gap, and a bright star touching an end are removed;
    • the line is a total-least-squares fit; each end is put where the ridge falls to half its level (the true end of a uniform streak blurred by the PSF);
    • the streak is traced once more from the middle of the fitted line, whose direction is better than the seed's;
    • it is kept if it is at least 10 px and 8 cross-section σ long, half covered, with a total signal-to-noise ratio of 10, and if at least half of the bright peaks on it are ridges (a row of stars is not a streak);
    • a streak narrower than the stars is flagged cosmic_ray.

    Only streaks bright enough to raise a peak above the detection threshold are found (about 5σ per PSF area). Fainter ones do not disturb the star list. Fayde does not search for them: an integration along the line (Radon transform; Nir, Zackay & Ofek 2018) costs 10 to 100 times everything here.

    3.8 Threshold and probabilities¶

    The threshold is set from the number of noise peaks the caller accepts per frame (default 1): the expected number of local maxima of filtered noise above u per pixel is bounded by min(P(Z > u), u·φ(u) / (4π·σk²)), the pixel count and the peak density of a continuous Gaussian field. On empty synthetic frames the bound is within 45% of the count for σk from 0.7 to 2 px. For a 4-megapixel frame the threshold is 5.0σ.

    p_star = (1 − local false discovery rate) × class factor. The local rate is the expected number of noise peaks within a quarter σ of a source's height over the number of sources seen there. A source at the threshold in an empty region of height gets a low probability; the same height among many real faint stars gets a high one.

    4. Position error¶

    The position is the maximum of the filtered image. Noise moves a maximum by (noise gradient) / (signal curvature). For white noise σn filtered by a Gaussian of σk, and a Gaussian star whose filtered profile has variance σt² = σp² + σk² and peak A:

    Var(∂n/∂x) = N² / (2σk²),  N = filtered noise;   curvature = A / σt²
σx = σt² / (√2 · σk · SNR),  SNR = A / N

    For a matched kernel (σk = σp) this is √2·σp / SNR, the Cramér–Rao bound for a Gaussian star in background noise (0.60 FWHM / SNR). Everything in it is measured per source.

    The star's own photon noise, with gain g given (N electrons): the same calculation with noise variance proportional to the star's profile gives

    Var = σp² · σt⁸ / (N · σk⁴ · (σk² + 2σp²)²)     = (16/9) · σp² / N  for a matched kernel

    the known result for a Gaussian-weighted centroid (Mortensen et al. 2010, eq. 6). Without a gain it is not modelled, and a floor of 0.05 px stands in. Saturated stars: 0.1 px + 3% of the radius of the saturated core (measured on synthetic frames).

    5. Measured¶

    Synthetic frames (fayde_detect::synth: pixel-integrated Gaussian or Moffat stars, sky and read noise, photon noise, streaks, hot pixels, cosmic rays, stray light, saturation). One laptop core.

    Quantity Result
    Speed, 4 Mpx frame, stars of σ 1.5 px 10 ms (2.4 ms/Mpx), one pass
    Speed, stars of σ 0.6 / 2.5 / 4 / 8 px 4 to 7 ms/Mpx (two or three passes; binned from σ 2.5 px up)
    Star width within 1% of the truth, σ 0.6 to 8 px
    Noise within 1% on sparse frames; reads up to 12% high when stars cover much of the frame
    Completeness every star above SNR 8; 21 of 28 between SNR 5 and 8
    Error / claimed error (robust spread; gain given) 0.8 to 1.25 for σ 0.9, 1.5, 2.6 px
    Error / Cramér–Rao bound, faint stars 0.8 to 1.3
    False stars on empty frames 3 in six 4 Mpx frames at the default (1 per frame asked)
    Hot pixels (60 per frame) flagged 60 of 60 at σ 0.7 and 1.5 px; 55 of 60 at σ 3 px (binned)
    Cosmic rays, 15 to 30 hits per star 0.3 to 0.6% of hits pass as faint stars at σ 1.5 px, 0.8% at σ 1.0 px; star width unaffected
    Stray light, 20 × sky from a corner 1 false star; without the local test, 33
    Streak ends within 2 px (0.2 px typical); faint streaks (5σ ridge) within 10 px
    Saturated stars, 3 to 300 × saturation, with bleed trails centre within 0.6 px; one detection each

    End to end (crates/fayde-api/tests/image_solve.rs, a synthetic sky through a known WCS, two streaks, hot pixels, saturated stars): worst sky error over the frame 0.11″ at 3″/px for well-sampled stars (0.035 px), 0.08″ undersampled, 0.49″ for seeing-limited stars 7 px wide. The solver's fitted error scale is 1.01 on the well-sampled frame: the claimed errors are the real ones.

    Real frames: 109 frames whose stars are points (two pixel scales; 3 to 10 s exposures, stars of FWHM 2.2 to 3.3 px, 4 to 540 hot pixels and radiation hits per frame, up to 4 streaks or cosmic-ray tracks in most frames), solved from the image file alone against UCAC4 with the header pointing (2° radius) and a scale prior:
    Solved 109 of 109
    Detection time 2.0 ms per frame (median)
    Matched stars 181 (median)
    Residual 0.40 px RMS (median)
    Error scale fitted by the solver 1.58 (median): on these frames the claimed errors are too small by that factor, with or without the gain

    Two of those frames shaped the method:

    • One, taken with a warm detector, has 300 radiation hits to 36 stars among its bright compact sources. It is what the widest-mode rule (§3.3) was made for.
    • One is a crowded field whose stars are smeared two to one by a drift. With narrowness judged against the stars' mean width, its 36 brightest stars were classed as cosmic rays (117 "sharp" sources; 7 once judged against the stars' narrow axis), and with the brightest stars missing the solver's search, which starts from them, found nothing although 417 of the remaining sources matched the catalogue. It now solves with 451 matched stars.

    The detector this one replaced (after SExtractor: clipped-mode mesh, pixel cleaning, matched filter, connected pixels, windowed centroids) was run on the same 109 frames with the same solver and priors: 105 solved, median residual 0.79 px, error scale 2.65, about 20 ms per frame. On the 105 frames both solve, the field centres agree to 0.19″ (median); on the one where they differ by 10″, this detector's solution has a residual of 1.5 px and the other's 5 px.

    6. Limits¶

    1. Faint streaks (under about 5σ per PSF area) are not found (§3.7).
    2. With stars of FWHM under about 2.5 px, multi-pixel cosmic-ray clumps of signal-to-noise ratio under about 30 cannot be told from faint stars by a 3 × 3 shape: in the worst synthetic case (3000 hits, 200 stars of FWHM 2.4 px, stray light and streaks together) 8% of the hits pass as faint stars. They are distractors the solver is built for.
    3. The claimed position errors assume a Gaussian core and white noise. On real frames the solver's error scale shows them 1.5 times too small; the cause (pixel response, PSF shape, pointing jitter) has not been separated.
    4. Undersampled stars (σ under 0.7 px): errors about 1.3 times the claim.
    5. One thread. The filter and the scan split into strips without changing the result.
    6. FITS images only (8 to 64-bit integers and floats); tile-compressed images are not read.
    7. Trailed stars (tracking a satellite) are out of scope here. The streak tracer does find star trails, and turning them into sources with Frame::trail is a possible extension.
