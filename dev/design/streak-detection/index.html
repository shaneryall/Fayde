    Trails and streaks in one detector¶

    This design extends fayde-detect (detection.md) to frames whose stars are trails: a straight-trail pass (§4, measured in §7) and empirical kernels for curved paths (§8). Literature: docs/research/literature-review.md §7.4 and §8. Sources: docs/references.md.

    1. Goal¶

    One detector, fayde-detect, that takes any frame from an imager pointed at the sky and returns what the solver needs, whatever moved during the exposure:

    Frame Stars Other things in it Fayde
    Sidereal tracking, or a spacecraft holding its attitude points satellite or debris streaks, cosmic-ray tracks handled; streaks only above about 5σ per PSF area
    Tracking a satellite, a slew, a drift trails, all the same length and direction the tracked object (a point), other satellites (streaks), cosmic rays handled for straight trails at least 7 FWHM long (§7)
    Either, with a faint satellite as above a streak below the per-pixel noise not found

    In trails mode the output adds the frame's star trail with its evidence, point-like sources (among them the tracked object), and per-source errors along and across the trail. The constraints are the point pass's (detection.md §1): no parameter the user must know, the same bits on every machine, no unsafe, no new dependency. Its cost is in §7.

    The straight-trail detector fits a common length and direction across the frame. The empirical kernel can measure a shared curved path or field rotation when it learns a reliable template (§8). When Fayde detects trails but cannot establish a consistent model, the report records the reason and the detector uses its point measurements.

    2. What exists¶

    The point pass, fayde-detect's pipeline for point sources, applies a Gaussian matched filter of the measured width, estimates the background and noise of the filtered image on a mesh, finds peaks, fits each with a log-quadratic (position, size, axis ratio, direction), and classes them (star, saturated, extended, sharp), then gives each a p_star from its class and its local false discovery rate. Streaks are traced from ridge-like peaks (axis ratio at least twice the stars') along a running least-squares line and reported with their ends; every peak on one is kept out of the star list. A drift that smears every star a little is handled by judging shape against the frame's own stars (detection.md §3.3). 109 of 109 real point-source frames solve, and point-pass detection takes a median 2.2 ms per frame (§7).

    An earlier detector, fayde-trails (about 3,100 lines, not merged), had:

    • a line matched filter computed directly (cost proportional to pixels × trail length), with an AVX2 path and a thread pool;
    • a clip loop, min(image, 2 · line mean), iterated so that compact bright sources do not leak into the line response;
    • the frame's trail from the flux-weighted second moments of connected components: L = √(12 · (λmajor − λminor)), direction from the major axis, with a verdict (Trails, Compact, Ambiguous, PriorMismatch, NoGeometry) and an optional prior that selects the population.

    On 74 real frames tracking satellites, measured against a reference WCS per frame: with a header prior its source lists solved 74 of 74; without one it refused 2 frames as ambiguous and the other 72 solved. Median center error 0.31 px against 0.50 px for fayde-detect's streak table used as a source list; 25 catalog matches against 16. Those figures predate its change to portable math.

    The solver accepts Frame::trail (length, angle). A source flagged truncated counts as evidence with a gate stretched along the trail but is never fitted or used as a seed. The verifier can add a variance along one direction to a gate (verify.rs, an extra term in quadrature). Everything else is isotropic: Source::sigma_px is one number, a match carries one σ, a fitted pair one weight, and the WCS covariance one normal matrix shared by both coordinates (verify.rs, fit.rs). Sources whose position falls outside the image are dropped (solve.rs).

    The point pass alone solves 9 of 84 frames tracking satellites (§7), so a second pass is needed. It can't reuse fayde-trails, whose costs (unsafe, a thread pool, cost growing with trail length) conflict with the workspace's rules.

    3. What the literature gives¶

    Detail and reading levels in the literature review, §8. The facts this design rests on:

    1. A trail needs its own filter. The matched filter for a trail is the PSF convolved with the segment. Its signal-to-noise ratio over a point filter's tends to √(L / (2√π·σ)) for L ≫ σ (Nir, Zackay & Ofek 2018). The LSST trailing-loss fits (Jones, SMTN-003) agree:
    Trail length / FWHM 2 5 10 20
    Gain, exact for a Gaussian PSF in white noise (computed) 0.12 mag 0.60 1.00 1.39
    Gain, long-trail limit √(L / 1.505 FWHM) 0.15 mag 0.65 1.03 1.40
    Gain, from SMTN-003's fits (point a = 0.420, b = 0.003; trail a = 0.761, b = 1.162) 0.18 mag 0.60 0.97 1.34

    Below about 2 FWHM the point filter gives up little; that is where the point pass stays. 2. The filter must be aimed. For long trails the matched response falls with an angle error Δθ as (√π / 2z)·erf(z), z = L·|Δθ| / 4σ: 2% lost at Δθ = σ/L, 7.7% at 2σ/L, 25% at 4σ/L. A length off by a factor k keeps 1/√k (a factor of 2 loses 29%, in the long-trail limit; 26% computed at L = 40σ), so lengths are measured, not taken from a coarse grid. (Derived; checked numerically. No published closed form was found.) 3. One trail per frame is cheap to find. The median orientation and major-axis length of bright elongated components gives it, and the median ignores a satellite streak or cosmic rays (Danescu & Turcu 2026: astrometry.net went from 15 to 39 of 40 frames solved once the image was filtered with that trail). The second moments give the length only for whole, evenly lit trails and a round PSF; components cut by the threshold read short. 4. An aimed box costs approximately O(1) per pixel. A running sum along translates of one digital line (Soille, Breen & Jones 1996) costs two additions per pixel whatever the length. It is not translation invariant: the pixels a window covers change with its phase along the digital line, so the effective length and centering depend on the angle. The Gaussian applied after it hides most of that; how much is left has to be measured against a direct filter. 5. The error of a trail is not round. For a Gaussian PSF convolved with a segment (Vereš et al. 2012; erf model as corrected by Bouquillon et al. 2017), when the background dominates, the error along the trail grows as L and across it as √L; when the star dominates, the error across is a static star's and the error along grows as √L (Bouquillon et al. 2017, Cramér–Rao bounds, for the model's own parameters; a fit with more nuisance parameters does worse). A brightness slope along the trail needs a tilt term (Blake et al. 2026); tracking jitter is common to every star in a frame, so an empirical profile stacked from bright trails beats the ideal model (Kouprianov 2008). 6. Faint streaks are found by multiscale line sums. Line integrals at a subsampled set of positions (spacing L/2 along the line), angles and dyadic lengths, built bottom-up (Brandt & Dym 1999; Ofir et al. 2020), about 8N responses per length and O(N log N) in all. For the mean of w·L independent pixels the noise threshold from K tests is σ·√(2 ln(K/δ) / (w·L)) (Ofir et al. 2020). Dyadic lines stray from true lines by up to log₂ n / 6 px for an image side n (Karpenko & Ershov 2021): 1.7 px at n = 1024. 7. One acceptance rule serves every line, as long as every test is counted. The number of false alarms, K · P(noise ≥ observed) (von Gioi et al. 2010 and the a-contrario school; Grosjean & Moisan 2009 for linear filters in Gaussian noise), is a union bound: it needs no independence between tests, but it needs valid null probabilities and every search counted. Clipping, masking, fitted-star subtraction, data-chosen geometry and endpoint optimization all change the null, so a whole pipeline has to be calibrated on simulated frames, not only the formula. 8. Point against segment is not a regular test. Length 0 is on the boundary of the parameter space and the angle is undefined under it (Self & Liang 1987; Davies 1987). Wilks's χ² and the usual BIC approximation do not apply; penalties calibrated by simulation do. 9. Others found these vetoes useful: a streak as wide as the PSF (narrower is likely a cosmic ray: Bektešević & Vinković 2017); the response across against along (Han et al. 2025); a centroid that moves when the model is fitted means a blend (Blake et al. 2026); a trail within L/2 of the edge is cut (Sharma et al. 2023 discard it; §4.6 uses it).

    4. Method¶

    image
  │ A. point pass (unchanged): filter, mesh, peaks, fits, classes, bright streaks
  │ B. trail census: one straight star trail common to the frame? → points | trails | refused
  │ C. segment pass (trails mode): masks, aimed segment filter, seeds
  │ D. one fit per trail on raw pixels: shared length, angle, width; position, flux, background free
  │ E. classes from a trail model and a point model: star, point, sharp
  ▼
stars (x, y, σ, σ along, σ across, flux, p_star) + streaks + point-like sources + frame trail

    The trail pass runs unless it is turned off (--no-trails). When the point pass binned the image, B uses a full-resolution point pass, saved before binning or run again. A frame that ends as points or refused keeps the point pass's star list bit for bit (the test point_frames_are_untouched in crates/fayde-detect/tests/trails.rs). An empirical kernel (§8) can still take a frame over: a field-rotation kernel after a points census, a shared curved template after a refusal, or over an accepted straight trail under the stronger conditions in §8. The frame's mode is then kernel.

    4.1 Trail census (B)¶

    Two estimates that fail in different ways; trails mode only when they agree.

    1. The first estimate comes from shapes. The filtered image is cut into 8-connected components of pixels above 4σ. A component votes when its peak is not masked and is at least 6σ, its variance ratio is at least 2.25 and it is at least 3 px long. Its vote is its direction and L = √(12·(λmajor − λminor)), weighted by its peak signal-to-noise ratio capped at 30, so one bright satellite cannot outvote fifty stars. The votes go into a circular histogram of the axis (an axis has no sign) in 36 bins of 5°, smoothed with weights 1, 2, 1. The largest mode gives θ₁, the weighted mean axis of the votes within 12.5° of it. A second mode more than 15° away, with at least 5 votes and half the first mode's weight, is a second population. L₁ is the weighted median length of the mode's votes whose peaks reach 18σ with no saturated pixel, or of all the mode's votes when fewer than 3 do. Taking the median length of the components follows Danescu & Turcu 2026 and fayde-trails; the weights and the 18σ choice are Fayde's. Components within 2 px of the edge do not vote for the length.
    2. The second comes from pixel pairs: the autocovariance of the filtered image. Unmasked pixels above 3σ serve as anchors; at least 50 are needed, and large sets are thinned to about 5,000. Each partner counts as the ratio of its signal-to-noise ratio to its anchor's, at most 2, and a masked partner counts as 0. Masked are the frame's mask, a zone around traced streaks that are not star trails, and pixels at 0.8 of saturation or more, grown by 3 px. The direction θ₂ is found coarse to fine: 36 directions at a lag of four trail widths (less for short trails), refined with a parabola, then the lag doubled up to L₁/2 with 17 angles a quarter of a width apart at that lag. A field of identical trails gives an elongated central peak; along θ₂ its profile is a triangle of half-width L blurred by a Gaussian of √2·σₜ (σₜ the trail width with the filter's), plus a near-flat floor from the pairs of different stars. L₂ is the L of the best fit of that model (amplitude and floor fitted linearly for each trial L from L₁/2 to 2L₁ in steps of 0.25 px), not where the profile reaches the floor.

    The decision (census.reason in the report says which rule decided):

    Outcome Rule
    points No vote; fewer than 5 votes in the mode; under half the weight in the mode (without a prior); elongated components carrying under 5% of the capped weight of all bright components; or L₁ under 7 FWHM across the trail (real frames meant to show points drift by up to 6.8 FWHM, §7)
    trails θ₁ and θ₂ within max(3σₜ/L₁, 0.08 rad), lengths within 20%, the straightness check passes, and the refit below accepts the geometry
    refused Two populations; the estimates disagree; the trails change across the frame; trails longer than the frame (no length votes, or fewer than 3 whole trails while at least 75% of the votes touch the edge); more than four round sources of the stars' width per trail vote; fewer than 50 anchors, or no fit of the second estimate with a positive amplitude; or the refit rejects the geometry

    The straightness check cuts the frame into 3 × 3 regions. A region with at least 4 votes gives an angle, and with at least 5 whole, unsaturated votes reaching 18σ a length. The frame is refused when a region's angle is off by more than twice the angle tolerance, or its length by more than 50%. With fewer than 2 regions measured, Fayde refuses the frame if fewer than 3 trails fit, or if the spread of their fitted angles exceeds max(2w/L, 0.05) rad.

    In the refit, the frame's length, angle and width are the medians of up to 15 of the brightest isolated whole trails, each fitted with all three free (at most 30 steps, χ²/dof at most 4); the census's values stand when none settles. When at least 3 fits, and over half of all attempts, come out more than 20% shorter than both the census length and their components' lengths, the geometry is rejected: such fits follow part of a curve.

    When radiation hits crowd the frame, or no trail emerges while at least 5 bright components are long and narrow (axis ratio 4 or more), the census is retried once on a cleaned image, with only its anchors and votes changed (§7 lists the cleaning steps).

    An angle prior (--trail-angle, Python trail_angle) selects a mode within 15° of it and then waives the share and second-population rules; the geometry is still measured from the image, as in fayde-trails. Detection takes no length, and no prior from the instrument profile or the header. In refused, the point-pass star list is returned. The report's census holds both estimates, the vote counts and shares, the elongated share, and the number of regions with their worst angle and length deviations; trail holds the refined geometry with its errors.

    4.2 Segment pass (C)¶

    1. A point convolved with a segment kernel is a segment-shaped response, so every hot pixel and cosmic-ray hit would become a faint false trail. The filter's input is masked, set to zero on the point pass's sharp detections and cosmic-ray tracks (when it ran at full resolution), pixels that are sharp at full resolution (sharp_in_raw) and undefined pixels. Saturated pixels stay in the filter and are left out of the fits. Traced streaks that are not star trails are subtracted with an erf model first. The raw image is kept for the fit (D).
    2. The aimed filter is a running sum along lines at θ, taken on sheared rows and interpolated between rows, with fractional ends weighted, so that its length is the physical L at every angle (the running box of Soille, Breen & Jones 1996 uses digital lines instead). Then a Gaussian of the trail's width (σ between 0.6 and 4 px). A unit test checks the box against sums along the line.
    3. For a smooth stationary Gaussian field of unit variance, the density of maxima above a high level u tends to (√det Λ / 2π)·u·φ(u), where Λ is the covariance of the field's gradient (Adler & Taylor 2007). The point pass's threshold is the smaller of that bound for a Gaussian kernel and the pixel tail. For the aimed filter, Λ comes from the model profiles along and across the trail, sampled every 0.25 px; along a long trail it scales as 1/(√π·σ·L). On frames whose sky is clipped at a floor the automatic threshold is at least 5σ. The bound is checked on empty frames for the point pass only.
    4. Filtering a trail with a box of its own length gives a blurred triangular response along the trail with its maximum at the trail's center, so each maximum is a seed near the center. A maximum within L/2 along the trail and 1.5 filter widths across of a brighter one is dropped; at most 5,000 seeds are kept.
    5. The mesh measures noise from differences of pixel pairs set apart (mesh.rs). In trails mode the pairs are set more than a trail apart (L + 3σk for the filtered image, L + 6w for the fit's weights), so that two pixels of one trail are not paired and the noise is not underestimated.

    4.3 One fit per trail (D)¶

    1. The model is an erf along the trail (Bouquillon et al. 2017) times a Gaussian across, of variance w² + 1/12 for the pixel's width, evaluated at pixel centers on the raw image. The frame's L, θ and width are fixed; position, flux and a background plane (a gradient, for stray light) are free. Weights are 1 / (σ² + s / gain), with σ the mesh's sky noise and s the model's signal above the background plus any subtracted neighbor or streak light (the photon term only when the gain is known), recomputed at each step. On frames whose sky is clipped at a floor, a prior holds the background.
    2. The fit starts from the center of light along the line (halfway there on clipped-sky frames) and the seed's position across it. A fit that moves more than 1 px is started once more from where it ended.
    3. The fit takes at most 8 bounded Gauss–Newton steps (24 on clipped-sky frames). A step is capped at max(0.1·L, 1) px, and a step that reverses is halved. The fit converges when its step is under a tenth of the position error (between 0.001 and 0.05 px) and counts when it is under half. A fit that leaves its stamp, cannot be solved or does not settle is dropped, and so is a trail known no better than half a trail along (one FWHM on clipped-sky frames) or two widths across, or a point known no better than two widths either way. Fixed limits keep the work and the bits fixed.
    4. The errors come from the inverse normal matrix at the parameters returned; they are projected along and across the trail (σ_along, σ_across) and floored. An optional test (error_claims_cover_the_truth) checks them on simulated frames: for matched stars away from the edge, 58 to 78% of the residuals within 1σ and at least 86% within 2σ, on each axis.
    5. Fits that converge within 1.5 widths of each other are merged when both fit well (χ²/dof at most 2). The others are fitted again with their neighbors' models subtracted where their light reaches the stamp; the first fit stays if the second fails.

    4.4 Classes (E)¶

    Each seed is fitted as a trail. It is also fitted as a point (the PSF, L = 0) when 40% of its light along the line lies within 2w + 1 px of its brightest column, or its trail fit has χ²/dof above 2. The two are compared on a common stamp centered on the fitted trail:

    Model Free Class (morphology) To the solver
    Star trail: the frame's L, θ, width position, flux, background star yes
    Point: the PSF position, flux, background point no; reported
    Point narrower than a star can be sharp no

    With d = ½(χ²point − χ²trail) on that stamp, the trail's probability against a point is p_trail = 1 / (1 + e^(−d)) (equal priors): a seed with p_trail of at least 0.5 is a star, otherwise a point. A point whose brightest pixel, less 3σ, holds more than 2.5 times the share of light a star puts in its brightest pixel is sharp (a hot pixel or a cosmic ray). p_star is (1 − local false discovery rate) × p_trail. Likelihood ratios between these models do not follow χ² (§3, item 8), so p_trail is a relative weight, not a calibrated probability.

    The classes describe shape, not what the object is: a point in a trailed frame is often the tracked object but can be anything moving with it. A cosmic-ray track that lies along the trail can pass as a star.

    4.5 Streaks¶

    The point pass traces streaks before the census, with no trail direction: its seeds are peaks elongated at least twice as much as the stars. After the census, a traced streak is removed with its sources when it looks like a star trail (within 3·max(2w/L, 0.05) rad of the trail, at most 3 trails long, not a cosmic ray) or a chain of star trails (within 12° and uneven along its length); the aimed filter's seeds then measure that light. Other streaks are subtracted before the trail pass, and a seed on one is kept only if its fit has χ²/dof of at most 1.5.

    4.6 Trails cut by the edge¶

    A trail whose outer end lies beyond the edge has a centroid biased by up to L/2 along it when it is measured as a blob. The fit holds the frame's L and θ fixed and fits the visible pixels, so it places a cut trail's center where the whole trail's would be, which can fall outside the image (on clipped-sky frames such sources are dropped). A trail is flagged truncated when its center is closer to an edge than half the trail's length projected toward that edge plus two widths. The solver scores a truncated source with a gate stretched along the trail but neither seeds nor fits with it, and drops a source whose center is outside the image (§2).

    On a square frame whose trails are a tenth of its width, about one star in eight is cut (12.4% averaged over directions, 10 to 13.6% depending on the direction).

    6. Integration choices and prior art¶

    Most of this is known technique put together: a segment-shaped PSF fitted with its neighbors' models subtracted is PSF photometry (Stetson 1987) with a longer PSF, and Houtz & Frueh 2021 and Sharma et al. 2023 already detect trails before plate solving. For one part, the survey (§8 of the literature review) found no antecedent, though that doesn't show none exists: the census's refusal rule, a segmentation-based estimate checked against a segmentation-free one, with regional straightness, and no trails mode unless they agree.

    • Cost: trails mode takes a median 40 ms per frame, against 2.2 ms for the point pass alone and 3.0 ms with the census on point-source frames. The fits are most of it.
    • 26 of the 84 frames are not in trails mode (14 called points, most with trails under 20 px; 12 refused, mostly because the two estimates disagree on real trails, whose brightness is not uniform along them), and 18 stay unsolved. The census also misses faint long trails: a 160 px trail spreads a star's light so thin that it may not stand out in the point filter the census votes from.
    • The census refuses two trail populations of comparable weight unless an angle prior selects one, and refuses the geometry when too few trails have both ends in the frame.
    • For straight trails, the solver gets one isotropic error per source, the larger of the errors along and across the trail, so the error across the trail is overstated.
    • A cut trail's error is the fit's own: the error of the frame's shared length and angle is not carried as one term common to every cut trail. On real frames, 411 cut trails had a spread along the trail of 1.65 times the claimed error, against 1.25 for whole trails.
    • Trails that nearly coincide are not split by a joint fit. Where fitted neighbors overlap, a seed is fitted again with their models subtracted; the first fit stays if the second fails.
    • The straight-trail fit has no term for brightness that changes along the trail.
    • Streaks fainter than about 5σ per PSF area are not found.

    8. Empirical kernels for curved star paths¶

    Tracking, rolling, or wobbling during an exposure can spread each star along a curve. A straight-trail filter can lock onto fragments of the path, giving the solver too few stars and positions displaced from their exposure-averaged coordinates.

    How it works¶

    1. Training reuses the census components and the census's defect mask. For a shared path, it keeps up to 64 bright, isolated, unsaturated components away from the frame edge, with lengths and orientations in the dominant mode. Their cutouts are background-subtracted with the mesh, normalized by flux and registered by their centers of light, then aligned twice to the current mean using subpixel correlation peaks. The template is their clipped, noise-weighted mean, recentered on its own light. This empirical PSF follows the approach of DAOPHOT and PSFEx. Its origin represents the exposure-averaged position of the star.
    2. When the straight-trail census accepts or refuses a dominant trail, Fayde measures the template's center of light in 2 px slices along its major axis and uses the empirical template when the path's RMS deviation from the best straight line is at least 0.5 px. When the census has already accepted one straight trail, the template overrides it only if it stacks at least 15 stars and bends by at least 1 px. On real frames tracking satellites, templates of 6 to 11 stars bent by 0.6 to 3 px moved solutions by up to 1.7 px from the straight-trail fits, which agreed with Gaia to 0.05–0.1 px. Every template must stack at least 10 stars: its center of light is every position's origin, so its error moves the whole solution (a template of 5 stars on a sparse simulated frame put the solution 2.6 px off). The sum of distances between slices estimates the path length. Evidence of field rotation can also select kernel mode when the census finds no common direction.
    3. The background-subtracted full-resolution image is correlated with the taps that hold 99% of the template's light. Undefined pixels, hot pixels, and cosmic-ray hits are masked with the trail pass's mask. Peaks are found with the mesh and the point pass's peak finder, using an equivalent Gaussian scale from the template's gradient covariance, and are visited in descending correlation amplitude.
    4. Each fit starts from a quadratic peak interpolation and refines the position and flux against the template in the image. The fitted signal must clear the noise threshold and agree with the template across its support. Each accepted source is subtracted in image space. Testing the correlation on the residual image applies the predicted autocorrelation of every brighter source with its actual subpixel shift and mask, which suppresses its sidelobes. A second correlation pass recovers peaks that were hidden by bright neighbors. This is a bounded, CLEAN-style subtraction.
    5. The flux is the matched-filter amplitude, and the position errors come from the template's gradient Fisher matrix. The weights include photon noise when the gain is available and an allowance for template mismatch. Each source's p_star comes from the local false discovery rate. The report gives the template size, training count, path length, bend, and region count. An image solve that abstains with time and hypotheses left retries with point detections.

    Field rotation and limits¶

    For small field rotations, elongated components trace tangents to circles about a common center. The center is fitted from their directions, and their lengths must scale with radius. Samples stacked across the middle of isolated trails give a common transverse profile, which is used with a Gaussian-smoothed longitudinal segment along a quadratic approximation to the local arc. The grid spacing limits the change in endpoint displacement within a region to about one pixel, or about 2.4 px at fast rotation, where the spacing reaches its 32 px minimum. Each fit evaluates the template at the candidate's position.

    The rotation model assumes a single center in the frame and an approximately uniform angular sweep. The shared template models one stable shape. Compact wobble that the census reads as point-like follows the point path. Large background variations and overlapping trails can still hide faint stars. The dense correlation is about 2–4 × 10¹⁰ multiply-adds on a 4096 × 4096 frame with a large template (several seconds), and rotation mode builds a local template for each candidate that reaches fitting. Next to a very bright star, residuals of a few percent of template mismatch could leave peaks along its path; such peaks must pass the fit-quality, signal-to-noise and separation tests, and none has been seen on the test frames.
